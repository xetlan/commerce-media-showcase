Identity model
anonymous_id stitched to user_id on login or known-customer identification. Cross-device, cross-session attribution is the default behavior, not a bolt-on. A user who sees the Hero banner on mobile Tuesday and converts on desktop Friday is one funnel completion, not two unrelated events.

Custom events fired by the campaign-tracking layer

  • placement_exposure — fires on viewport entry of any placement zone; carries placement_id, placement_type, placement_position, page_template, fold_position, product_brand, sku
  • placement_click — fires on click of any sponsored slot; placement properties + sku + product_brand
  • add_to_cart · checkout_started · order_completed — standard ecommerce events with sku / revenue / currency
  • frustration_signal — emitted on rage-click, dead-click, slow load >3s, or JS error within a sponsored zone. Aggregated per placement_id to compute a Frustration Score proxy.
Why this scales. Every dashboard below is a PA query against this stream. The retailer maintains no Merchandising catalog. No mapping needs to be redrawn for a new placement type — it drops in as a new placement_type enum value and the downstream queries continue to work. Identity stitching is automatic. The same event stream feeds A/B test analysis, retention cohorts, and audience destinations to ad platforms — see the "What enriched PA data does here" section below the dashboards.

Why this campaign existed

ZX 500 RM is a heritage Originals franchise. Spring is its highest-volume buying window — roughly 60% of annual unit sales for the franchise occur Mar–May. Adidas's Q1 ROAS on CStore had drifted down 7% to 3.2x, below the brand's commerce-media benchmark of 3.6x. The Spring Drop introduces five new colorways; the campaign was built around the franchise launch with three strategic intents: (1) reverse the Q1 ROAS decline, (2) reclaim share-of-shelf in athletic footwear against the Nike Air Max franchise, and (3) build brand-search momentum heading into the summer drop cycle.

Primary objectives — set at kickoff

  1. Revenue — $475,000 attributed; ROAS ≥ 3.70x.
  2. Growth — +15% MoM in attributed revenue.
  3. Audience quality — ≥35% of revenue from High-intent + Loyalty segments combined.
  4. Halo — organic Adidas activity ≥+15% during campaign window vs prior period (PDP views, brand-search queries, organic ATC).
  5. UX operational — no placement with Frustration Score >0.35 left un-investigated by end of campaign.

Planned inventory mix & creative strategy

  • Hero banner (35% of budget): top-of-funnel awareness. "Spring Drop" lifestyle creative, 14-variant carousel of new colorways.
  • Search carousel (26%): mid-funnel discovery on query intent. Product-grid creative anchored on $80 starting price.
  • Category PLP slot (20%): in-category discovery for engaged browsers. Static row 1 (positions 1–4) on athletic footwear PLP.
  • PDP cross-sell (13%): consideration / complete-the-look. "Often bought with" position, 3-SKU bundle creative.
  • Checkout upsell (4%): conversion-stage bundle save 15% on second pair.
  • Branded landing page (2%): campaign hub — full ZX 500 franchise lookbook + 5 new colorways.

Audience & targeting strategy

Prioritize Loyalty (22% of planned exposures) and High-intent shoppers (18%) for conversion-weighted placements (Checkout upsell, PDP cross-sell). Use Lapsed customers (32% planned) and First-time visitors (28%) for awareness placements (Hero, Search). Halo measurement window set to T-30 vs T+30 against prior-period baseline (same retailer, same product franchise, seasonality-adjusted).

⚡ What enriched PA data does here that a Merchandising-led setup struggles with

Same dashboards above. Same numbers. Different underlying capability — and a wider set of next moves.

Cross-device identity stitching

A user who sees the Hero banner on mobile Tuesday and converts on desktop Friday is one funnel completion, not two unrelated events. Session-anchored attribution (the Merchandising default) would have classified it as one exposure plus one unattributed organic purchase. Stitched user_id credits it correctly.

Behavioral cohorts → audience destinations

"Users with ≥3 Hero exposures and 0 ATCs in 14 days" is a one-click cohort. Auto-synced to Meta, Google, retailer's CRM and email. The same insight that surfaces the underperforming Hero in D2 funds the next retargeting wave automatically.

Retroactive analysis

Decide next month that exposure-to-search is a useful metric? PA replays the historical event stream — no re-instrumentation. Merchandising would require the metric to be pre-defined in a catalog field or mapping rule.

Funnel breakdown by any property

Filter the Hero-banner → ATC funnel by device_class, loyalty_tier, time-of-day, creative variant, geo, or any combination. Every breakdown is a click. Merchandising's funnels are fixed paths defined at the mapping level.

A/B variant as a user property

Spring Drop ran two Hero creative variants (A: lifestyle imagery, B: product grid). experiment_variant stamped on every downstream event. Result: variant B converted 1.8× variant A — surfaced in a 30-second PA query. No separate experiment-platform integration required.

Retention curves on exposed cohorts

Did exposed users return more often than non-exposed in the 30 days after the campaign? PA shows the retention curve natively, by cohort and by placement_type. Halo measurement gets a behavioral counterpart — not just aggregate-volume lift but per-user repeat behavior.

Where PA gives ground vs Merchandising: there's no dedicated catalog-driven product hierarchy UI, no native Zoning heatmap renderer, and Frustration Score must be computed from event signals rather than read from a managed score. For most commerce-media use cases the trade is favorable — flexibility and identity stitching beat a pre-built UI — but a retailer running tens of thousands of SKUs with hierarchical merchandising taxonomies will still want Merchandising for catalog-side workflows.

📖 How to read this retrospective

The campaign brief at the top told you what was planned. The seven panels showed what happened. The superpowers section above explained what makes this PA-only setup distinctive. This final section explains how to read the gap between plan and result — and what to demand before the next renewal.

Executive read — 60 seconds

Across 2.4M exposure sessions and $128K of media spend over 30 days, Adidas pulled $503,580 in attributed revenue (ROAS 3.93x) plus an estimated $94,200 of organic halo revenue (effective ROAS 4.67x). Growth is real (+18% MoM) and concentrated in mobile search carousel. But ~$114K of monthly revenue — roughly 23% of the total — comes from three underperforming placements that consume 30% of impression budget. Fixing the bottom three to portfolio median is worth ~$521K annualized. To top quartile, ~$1.04M. The single most defensible request to make at renewal is to reallocate hero-banner inventory to checkout upsell and mid-scroll PDP cross-sell — placements that produce $924 and $431 of revenue per 1,000 exposures versus $46 from the hero.

Reading each panel — Adidas perspective

D1 · Brand Performance Cockpit

The headline ROAS of 3.93x is your anchor for every internal commerce-media debate. Report it up; defend the budget with it. The +18% MoM growth and +0.4x ROAS-vs-benchmark advantage is the case for increasing the next inventory commitment, not holding flat. Share-of-shelf at 27% is context, not a controllable lever — it's how the retailer characterizes your category dominance, useful for narrative but not for negotiation.

D2 · Placement Breakdown — the single most important table on the page

Two columns matter most: CR (of clicks) tells you how well each placement converts attention; RPME tells you how productively each thousand impressions earns. RPME is the metric the retailer would not have surfaced if they wanted to hide weak placements — the fact that they did is a transparency signal worth rewarding in the relationship. Three placements at $264–$924 RPME versus three at $46–$421 means a ~20x productivity gap inside a single buyer's portfolio. The Frustration Score column (0.42 on hero banner, 0.38 on branded landing page) is your evidence of UX-level problems — if users are rage-clicking on a placement that carries your brand, your brand is what they remember.

D3 · Zone Heatmap

Adidas currently buys "Often bought with" (intensity 62) but is not in "You may also like" (intensity 87 — the highest-performing zone on the entire platform). That gap is the single biggest purchasable upside in this dashboard. The retailer has identified the best real estate and you are not yet in it.

D4 · Top 3 / Bottom 3

Treat the diagnostic column as more important than the rank. Checkout upsell at 14% CR is not a coincidence — it's high-intent context with relevant SKUs. The takeaway is not "buy more of what's working"; it's "the retailer has shown they know which formats convert. Negotiate access proportional to that intelligence."

D5 · Revenue Impact — the dashboard you take into the renewal meeting

The retailer has quantified $43,439/month of recoverable revenue across three placements — money Adidas is currently spending but not converting. Two of the three optimization decisions are flagged ✅ Proceed: Hero refresh (~$45K cost, $250K expected lift, ~6x ROI) and Category PLP retune (~$15K cost, $180K expected lift, ~12x ROI). The question to bring to renewal: "are these optimizations on the retailer's side as part of the package, or do they fall to Adidas?" That answer is worth more than any rate negotiation.

D6 · Yield & Inventory — read for your rank, not your decisions

Adidas is #2 by spend, #2 by ROAS, #1 by MoM growth. Nike outspends you by 45% but is growing at half your rate. That asymmetry is your single strongest negotiating chip — Adidas is the rising account in this retailer's commerce-media book, and you should be treated accordingly.

D7 · Audience & Halo — the under-priced panel

Two numbers matter: (a) 41% of revenue comes from 18% of exposures (the high-intent segment), which proves the retailer is delivering quality, not just volume; and (b) $94,200 of halo revenue Adidas is paying for but currently does not count in ROAS. Adopting the halo-inclusive methodology moves the headline number from 3.93x → 4.67x with zero change in actual performance. That's a free 19% on your board-deck number.

The implied result — what the data is telling you

Stripped of the visual chrome: Adidas's commerce media on this retailer is working but unevenly. Two-thirds of the budget produces 90% of the value. The bottom third — anchored by the hero banner — drags the portfolio CR down by ~1.5 percentage points and ~$43K/month of revenue. The retailer's Sense AI surfaces this; Adidas should know it too. ROI — where the money is

Three recoverable value pools, in order of confidence:

  1. Reallocation gain — high confidence, no investment. Move 30% of hero banner spend (~$12K/month, $144K annualized) to checkout upsell and mid-scroll PDP cross-sell. At those placements' current CRs (14% and 5.6%), expected revenue gain is $19K–$28K per month, or $228K–$336K annualized. Requires only an inventory-access conversation.
  2. Optimization lift — high confidence, retailer-funded if negotiated. If the retailer commits to the Hero refresh + PLP retune flagged ✅ in D5, combined annualized lift is ~$430K against ~$60K of work. The right negotiation puts that $60K on the retailer's side. Net to Adidas: $430K incremental revenue, zero incremental cost.
  3. Halo methodology — measurement upgrade only. Adopting halo-inclusive ROAS moves the reported number from 3.93x → 4.67x. No actual revenue change, but the reported metric supports a defensible 15–20% commerce-media budget uplift at the next planning cycle.

Combined credible upside Adidas can demand from this renewal: ~$430K of optimization-driven lift + $228K–$336K of reallocation lift, plus a methodology reset on the headline metric. Against the current $6.04M annualized run-rate, that's an additional $660K–$770K of incremental revenue without adding budget.

What Adidas should do — priority order

  1. Lock the renewal conversation to the next 14 days while ROAS is climbing and growth is +18% MoM. Leverage is at its highest point right now.
  2. Renewal-conversation leverage

Three asks land naturally from the data and the retailer cannot easily refuse them, because each is anchored in numbers from their own dashboards:

Ask 1 — Rate hold or modest decline on hero banner. It's underdelivering and the retailer's own dashboard says so (D2 + D5). Anchor: $46 RPME vs $264 portfolio average.
Ask 2 — Premium-rate access to mid-scroll PDP cross-sell, conditional on retailer-funded optimization commitments for the bottom three placements. Anchor: D3 intensity score of 87, vs 62 on the zone Adidas currently occupies.
Ask 3 — Performance-floor clause. If any placement drops more than 20% MoM in CR (the same threshold the retailer uses internally for renewal-risk flags, see D6), Adidas can reallocate that budget without penalty.

What's at risk if you don't act

The bottom three placements will not improve on their own. Current trajectory: ~$38K/month ($456K/year) of spend at sub-portfolio CR. Worse, the 0.42 Frustration Score on hero banner means a meaningful share of Adidas brand impressions correlate with a negative on-platform experience. Brand-equity erosion does not show up in this dashboard — it shows up six months later in unaided-awareness studies. Renewing without addressing the bottom three means paying full rate for inventory the retailer has already diagnosed as broken.

Questions to challenge the retailer on

  • Attribution definition. Is "attributed revenue" last-click, exposure-weighted, or first-touch? Different definitions give different numbers — ask which methodology produced the $503K, and whether the same is used to compare across brand partners.
  • Halo control window. Was the pre-campaign baseline the same season, or a year-ago period? Seasonality can produce a "halo" that isn't real.
  • Statistical significance on Branded landing page. The flag is borderline (p=0.04). How many weeks of data sit behind it? Would the retailer agree to re-read at p<0.01 before final decisions?
  • Checkout upsell fill rate of 65%. The retailer has unsold premium inventory on their highest-CR placement. Why isn't Adidas already in it?

Bottom line

The retailer has handed Adidas a fully-quantified, statistically-significant audit of its own commerce-media performance — including the parts that are underperforming. Most brand-side teams never see this level of granularity. The right response is not gratitude; it is to convert the transparency into commercial terms at renewal. Every dollar of recoverable value identified in this dashboard is a number Adidas can name in the negotiation room.

Brief for the next campaign (Summer Drop, planning starts now)

What Spring Drop's data tells you about how to scope the next franchise launch:

  1. Rebalance the inventory mix. Move 30% of Hero budget into Checkout upsell + mid-scroll PDP cross-sell at planning stage, not mid-flight. Hero stays in the plan as awareness, but at a lower CPM and smaller share.
  2. Set audience-quality targets higher. Spring Drop hit 69% High-intent + Loyalty share against a 35% goal. Recalibrate the next campaign's audience-quality KPI to ≥55% — closer to actual delivery, harder to game.
  3. Move Frustration Score from "operational watch" to "kill-switch". Any placement above 0.35 by week 2 of campaign auto-paused pending Replay diagnosis. Pre-negotiate this clause with the retailer.
  4. Lock halo methodology in advance. Spring Drop produced +24–31% halo, but the measurement was finalized post-hoc. For Summer Drop, the control window, baseline definition, and seasonality adjustment are agreed at kickoff and signed by both teams. Removes the "is the halo real?" debate from the result conversation.
  5. Set the Summer Drop revenue target at $610K (ROAS ≥4.0x). Spring delivered $503K at 3.93x; +21% revenue and +0.07x ROAS is a defensible step-up given the optimization commitments now negotiable at renewal.
